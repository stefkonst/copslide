the last straight: could we have beaten the cop?
SimulationDataLaunch
  1. On the last straight, our fastest riders were already as slippery as clothes on steel get (friction ≈ 0.10). Posture can't add much: rolling onto the side or moulding into the trough saves under 2 %.
  2. They were still slower than the cop: 0.81–0.84 s from the tube mouth to the lip vs his 0.70 s, because they came out of the tube at 13–14 km/h and he needed at least ~18 km/h.
  3. The difference is made in the turns. To come out of the tube at 18 km/h you have to leave the turns at about 12 km/h; our riders left them at 4–6 km/h.
  4. A "wannabe cop" (internet clip, full rain suit, same face-down twisted posture) did 0.75 s: a slick suit and that posture get you close to the cop.

Physics and assumptions
  • (all used below; the web page lists the same)
  • slide 30.0 deg slope (measured), lower straight ends at s = 11.05 m, the slope eases to the flat over 0.40 m (11.05-11.45), flat to the lip at 12.75 m, lip 0.55 m above the ground. Trough = circular arc, radius 0.375 m (0.75 m opening, measured). Covered tube 7.05-8.55 m.
  • body a rigid body sliding along the trough axis, treated as a point at its centre of mass (CoM) for the equation of motion. Feet-first riders: CoM 0.90 m behind the feet; the cop's vest (chest) is taken as his CoM (+-0.3 m, affects only where the slope->flat bend is felt). Mass cancels out except in air drag (75 kg used).
  • contact the body rests on the trough wall at its two outer edges (rigid shoulders/hips). For a contact width w in a trough of radius R the wall pushes at angle phi from straight down, sin(phi) = w / 2R, so the total pressing force is the weight component / cos(phi) ("wedging"). Contact width: lying flat (back or belly) 0.42 m (shoulders), on the side 0.24 m (body depth), in between w = 0.42|cos r| + 0.24|sin r|. A soft body that moulds into the curve wedges less: the rigid-edge value is an upper bound, 1.0 the lower bound.
  • friction mu = fabric on steel, constant along the stretch and with speed (Coulomb). mu_eff = mu * wedge factor is what the videos measure.
  • air drag 1/2 rho Cd A v^2, rho = 1.2 kg/m3, Cd*A = 0.12 m2 lying feet first, 0.40 m2 sitting up. Below 1 % of gravity at these speeds.
  • bend the slope->flat bend presses the body down by v^2 * curvature (curvature = 30 deg / 0.40 m): extra friction while turning flat.
  • flight leaves the lip horizontally; CoM 0.67 m above the ground; flight distance = v_exit * sqrt(2 h / g).
  • data riders: leading shoe at 9.0 m, 11.45 m and the lip, read frame by frame at 30 fps (+-0.047 s per interval), plus the logger's speed leaving the turns (+-20 %); the two are fitted jointly as one ride. Cop: torso out of the tube mouth and over the lip, effectively 15 fps (63 of 168 frames are duplicates): 0.70 +- 0.067 s. "Wannabe cop" internet clip (rain suit, same posture): 0.75 +- 0.07 s, read the same way.
  • limits friction of clothes on steel is at least ~0.10, so mu_eff >= 0.10 is imposed. Without it the fastest riders' fits drift to 0, i.e. the logger reads their speed leaving the turns ~10-15 % low; with the floor, the fit raises that speed instead (within its +-20 %).
