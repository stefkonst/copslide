Coming to rest: the median of three independent estimates (logger goes quiet, GoPro picture goes still, model), with their spread as the error line; "?" = they differ by more than 2 s, typically slow riders who crept to a stop. ○ = the model fitted to that ride. The two best-measured stretches, ranked separately

The full-descent ranking mixes a well-measured part (the turns) with a weak one (when a slow ride really ended). Here each stretch is ranked on its own, over the same distance for everyone. Left: the time through the turns, from the gyro, with both ends measured (no launch time needed: it can't be pinned from the logger alone). Right: the landing video's tube mouth → lip time, which only riders who reached the lip have, and which is where a rider like P13 shows up as fast. Hatched = the logger's turn signature was unreliable. ○ = the same turn time from the chest camera's own gyro and clock: where the two agree (most rides, within ~0.1 s) the turn time is solid. For a spinning rider (P13, P15) both sensors are fooled the same way, so agreement means nothing.

Why not one clock from the first turn to the lip? The turns are timed on the logger's clock, the lip on the landing camera's, and the landing clips were trimmed without a shared sync event. The logger shows no clean free fall or landing spike off the lip (the trough is bumpy and the rubber soft), so the two clocks can only be linked to about ±0.3–0.5 s. A sync cue that every camera and the logger record (the GO beep next to the landing camera, a clap or a flash) would make the whole descent measurable end to end next time.

How many g?

Waist logger during each ride (launch to the end). Bars: the peak sustained acceleration (smoothed at 3 Hz, what the whole body feels; 1 g = standing still). Dots: the hardest single knock against the steel. Nobody filmed a logger on the cop, so his value comes from the calibrated full-body simulation with a virtual logger at the waist. The simulation under-reads our riders by a factor 1.0–1.9 (◇ vs bars: its slide is perfectly smooth), so his estimate is scaled the same way: about 5 g, plausibly 4–7 g, at the bend where the slope flattens out.
