Work in progress (update 2). All runs synced and reviewed; statistics done. Still to come: the landing videos (where on the lower slide people stopped) and the final write-up.

Nineteen riders went down once each in normal clothes, three of them again in a cop suit. A waist logger and a chest GoPro each timed the slide's turns.

What we found so far

  1. Everyone was slow. Through the turns, riders in normal clothes did 1–5 km/h (median about 3 km/h): walking pace. The viral cop was going about 20 km/h.
  2. The measurement is solid. The waist logger and the chest GoPro timed each turn independently and agree within about 7 % (median). The differences between riders (about ±50 %) are real, not noise.
  3. Everyone's friction sits right at the "stall" point (μ 0.54–0.62, stall 0.58), where friction and gravity cancel. On that knife-edge, a small difference in clothing, posture or push-off turns into a big difference in speed. The cop had about half our friction.
  4. The cop suit made no consistent difference. P04 was faster in it, P05 slower, P06 the same. One rider's runs can differ by ±40 %, so a suit effect smaller than that can't be seen with one run each.
  5. Denim was slowest: riders in jeans did about 2.3 km/h through the turns, against about 4 km/h in other fabrics (7 riders in denim; suggestive, not conclusive).
  6. Riders knew how fast they were: their own speed ratings match the measurements closely.
