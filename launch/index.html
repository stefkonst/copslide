the last straight: could we have beaten the cop?
Whole-slide simulationLanding simulationAnalysisResults
  1. Our fastest riders came out of the tube at 10–13 km/h; the cop at 16–22 km/h. Over the same last 3.95 m (tube mouth → lip) they took 0.87–1.58 s, he took 0.70 s.
  2. On the last straight they were reasonably slippery (friction 0.18–0.37, well below the 0.58 stall line) — the cop may have been slipperier (anything from 0.10 to 0.40 fits his clip), but that alone can't close the gap.
  3. The gap is made in the turns. To come out of the tube as fast as the cop, P12 would have needed to leave the turns at ~16 km/h instead of ~4.
  4. Full-body physics from the top of the turns confirms it. Calibrated to each run, the 3-D ragdoll reproduces P12, Angel and Stef (Stef slides off the end slowly, at about 3 km/h, as on the video). The cop's run needs friction 0.15 all the way with a relaxed body: he leaves the turns at 13 km/h (ours at 6) and reaches the tube mouth twisted 82°, legs flying.
  5. The swing and twist in his video are a consequence of speed. At our riders' speeds the same body stays at the bottom and twists under 10°; at the cop's speed a relaxed body swings up the walls and twists 80–90° — like in his video. Held stiff, it twists only ~40°, so he was probably loose. A "wannabe cop" in a rain suit, lying like the cop, did 0.75 s.

Every video time is shown frame by frame in results/gate_checks. Earlier versions used a marker that perspective had put ~1 m too far down the trough and over-stated our riders' speeds by ~30 %.

Full-body physics (Rapier engine): 11 linked body parts, contacts and Coulomb friction with the real trough shape, flight off the lip and landing on the playground. Rolling, legs swinging and head knocks emerge from the physics — nothing is animated by hand.

Effect of each parameter

Each figure changes one setting and keeps the rest fixed.

Speed out of the tube

Expected: The time on the last straight depends mostly on how fast you come out of the tube; friction on the straight moves it much less. Only about 18 km/h or more gives the cop's 0.70 s. P12 came out at 12 km/h and needed 1.04 s.

Friction in the turns

Expected: Keeping our riders' straight friction, lowering the turn friction from 0.41 (ours) to 0.15 (the cop's) raises the speed out of the tube from 12.5 to 18 km/h and cuts the last straight from 0.98 to 0.76 s. The gap is made in the turns.

Muscle tone

Expected: A limp body (tone 0) twists about 60° at the tube mouth; a body held stiffer twists 18–44°. The time hardly changes (0.68–0.71 s). The cop's big twist on the video is what a fast, relaxed body does.

Body position

Expected: At the same 18 km/h out of the tube every position takes about the same time. Position does not explain the gap on the last straight.

Recorded runs vs the full-body physics

Each case is the 3-D ragdoll calibrated to that run (friction in the turns from the logger's turn time, on the straight from the video). On the video, the simulated head, pelvis and feet are drawn on top, placed along the slide with the same reference points used to time the video: if they sit on the rider, the timing is right.

Physics and assumptions
  • (all used below; the web page lists the same)
  • slide 30.0 deg slope (measured), lower straight ends at s = 11.05 m, the slope eases to the flat over 0.40 m (11.05-11.45), flat to the lip at 12.75 m, lip 0.55 m above the ground. Trough = circular arc, radius 0.375 m (0.75 m opening, measured). Covered tube 7.05-8.80 m; the open trough length (2.25 m, 2.0-2.5) is fitted from the riders' videos (trough_length.py).
  • body a rigid body sliding along the trough axis, treated as a point at its centre of mass (CoM) for the equation of motion. Feet-first riders: CoM 0.90 m behind the feet; the cop's vest (chest) is taken as his CoM (+-0.3 m, affects only where the slope->flat bend is felt). Mass cancels out except in air drag (75 kg used).
  • contact the body rests on the trough wall at its two outer edges (rigid shoulders/hips). For a contact width w in a trough of radius R the wall pushes at angle phi from straight down, sin(phi) = w / 2R, so the total pressing force is the weight component / cos(phi) ("wedging"). Contact width: lying flat (back or belly) 0.42 m (shoulders), on the side 0.24 m (body depth), in between w = 0.42|cos r| + 0.24|sin r|. A soft body that moulds into the curve wedges less: the rigid-edge value is an upper bound, 1.0 the lower bound.
  • friction mu = fabric on steel, constant along the stretch and with speed (Coulomb). mu_eff = mu * wedge factor is what the videos measure.
  • air drag 1/2 rho Cd A v^2, rho = 1.2 kg/m3, Cd*A = 0.12 m2 lying feet first, 0.40 m2 sitting up. Below 1 % of gravity at these speeds.
  • bend the slope->flat bend presses the body down by v^2 * curvature (curvature = 30 deg / 0.40 m): extra friction while turning flat.
  • flight leaves the lip horizontally; CoM 0.67 m above the ground; flight distance = v_exit * sqrt(2 h / g).
  • data riders: leading shoe at the tube mouth, the slope->flat bend and the lip (physical features only), read frame by frame at 30 fps (+-0.035 s, mouth up to +-0.1 s; results/gate_checks), plus the logger's speed leaving the turns (+-20 %); fitted jointly as one ride. Cop: torso out of the tube mouth and over the lip, effectively 15 fps (63 of 168 frames are duplicates): 0.70 +- 0.067 s. "Wannabe cop" internet clip (rain suit, same posture): 0.75 +- 0.07 s, read the same way.
  • limits mu_eff >= 0.10 (clothes on steel). The fits want riders leaving the turns ~30-50 % faster than the logger reads: the logger's turn-exit speed is likely low (it is a model value at the end of the turns).
