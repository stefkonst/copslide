Effect of each parameter

One choice changed at a time, the rest fixed (6 km/h push, on the back, normal clothes); same physics as above.

Push-off speed

Expected: A harder push adds little: from no push to 16 km/h, the speed out of the tube goes 8.4 → 10.0 km/h in normal clothes and 18.2 → 18.7 in the cop uniform. In normal clothes the rider still stops at 12.0 m; the cop uniform always shoots off. The turns take the push away again.

Friction coefficient (what the clothes set)

Expected: Speed falls steadily as friction rises towards the stall value tan 30° = 0.58; from about 0.35, the rider no longer reaches the end lip. Normal clothes (0.40) stop on the exit flat; the cop uniform (0.15) shoots off.

Starting position

Expected: Speed out of the tube: 7–10 km/h in normal clothes and 18 in the cop uniform, depending on the position. The clothes matter far more than the position, which mostly changes how the body twists.

Speed along the slide

Expected: Same push and position, only the clothes differ: the curves split in the turns and never come back together.

Physics: Rapier rigid-body engine; slide geometry from measured segment lengths; one friction coefficient for the whole slide; body held loosely (same muscle tone for everyone). Calibrated against real rides on the Landing simulation page and in Results.
